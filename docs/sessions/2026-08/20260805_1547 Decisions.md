# Conv Decisions - 2026-08-05

## 1. Adopt the client's band colours verbatim, as `brian-*` tokens
**Type:** Implementation
**Topics:** design-tokens, merge-brian

**Trigger:** Client asked for his band background on the course card; his hexes are off-palette (his own source calls them "one step past `bg-primary-light`").

**Options Considered:**
1. Inline the raw hexes as arbitrary classes
2. Add `--brian-band` / `--brian-band-line` to the existing `brian-*` namespace ← Chosen
3. Add a new `150`/`200` step to the `info` ramp

**Decision:** Tokens in the `brian-*` namespace, which already exists ([TAB-THEME], Conv 414) for verbatim client palette values.

**Rationale:** Follows an established pattern rather than inventing one, and labels provenance honestly. The `info` ramp is `100/300/500` across all six ramps; adding a 150 to one only would break that symmetry.

**Consequences:** Two new primitives + two bridge re-exports. The tokenisation guard test survives with only its token name changed.

## 2. Add a `band` presentation rather than restyling `CommunityAffiliation`
**Type:** Implementation
**Topics:** design-system, components

**Trigger:** The component has two consumers that place it very differently.

**Options Considered:**
1. Restyle the component wholesale to the band
2. Add `presentation: 'inline' | 'band'` ← Chosen
3. Port the client's separate `CommunityBand.tsx` as a second component

**Decision:** A presentation variant on the existing component.

**Rationale:** `CourseHeader` renders it **inline inside a dark image hero**, not as a bottom strip — a wholesale restyle would have put a pale blue strip inside the dark hero. A second component would re-fragment what Conv 425 deliberately consolidated.

**Consequences:** The hero is untouched; `tests/components/entity/CommunityAffiliation.test.tsx` guards the split from both sides.

## 3. Reverse the medallion on the /communities card; keep the band colour
**Type:** Strategic
**Topics:** design-system, communities

**Trigger:** User: "I may have overstepped… what does that image represent from a community point of view?"

**Options Considered:**
1. Keep the medallion for uniformity
2. Revert both the medallion and the band colour
3. Revert the medallion, keep the band colour ← Chosen

**Decision:** Medallion reversed; band colour retained.

**Rationale:** The medallion encodes "belongs to another community" — true on the course card, false where the logo is the card's own subject. The band colour carries no such claim; it was adopted on legibility (`#F1F9FF` on a 25px strip read as a hairline divider, not a band).

**Consequences:** The two catalog cards share a band blue but not a logo treatment, which is the correct asymmetry.

## 4. Community card leads with the logo over a scrimmed cover
**Type:** Strategic
**Topics:** design-system, communities

**Trigger:** User asked whether the community card should show the logo instead of the cover image, and what then happens to the image.

**Options Considered:**
1. Scrim the cover, bring the logo forward ← Chosen
2. Logo-led, drop the cover from the card entirely
3. Leave the structure, only settle the inset's shape

**Decision:** Cover behind the `/community/[slug]` hero's exact gradient (`rgba(0,0,0,0.7)→0.4`); logo forward as a centred 56px `rounded-12` squircle.

**Rationale:** Not a new idea — our own detail hero and the client's own community card both already do this, making our catalog card the only surface showing the cover undarkened with the logo demoted. The image keeps a job (atmosphere) instead of competing with identity. Squircle over circle because these are app-icon-square marks and a circle crops their corners.

**Consequences:** `/communities` hero cards restructured; three new assertions guard the scrim and the squircle.
